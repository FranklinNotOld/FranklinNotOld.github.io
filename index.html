I am a Ph.D. student at Purdue University, advised by Prof. Yiheng Feng. My research focuses on safe and interpretable autonomous driving: forecasting how surrounding road users will move, anticipating accidents before they happen, and grounding language and vision in world models that let a vehicle reason about what comes next.

Before Purdue, I was a research assistant at the State Key Laboratory of Internet of Things for Smart City, University of Macau, working with Prof. Zhenning Li and Prof. Chengzhong Xu.

I like ideas that cross disciplines — several of my models borrow from how human drivers perceive and reason — and the engineering it takes to make them work outside the lab. I have built connected-vehicle systems end to end and deployed them on real vehicles, and I enjoy untangling complex systems problems along the way.

Open to collaboration. I have several ideas in autonomous driving that need more compute than I currently have. If you would like to work together, or just exchange ideas, feel free to reach out.

Research · Purdue × INDOT

Work Zone Risk Prediction

A roadside system that predicts which vehicles are about to cut into a highway work zone: a 4 s time-to-collision screen, then an attention-LSTM that forecasts the next 4 s of each flagged vehicle. Field-tested at work zones in Indiana; ≈ 11 ms per prediction.

Connected Vehicles

Getting the warning into the car: a digital twin of the work zone feeds a roadside unit, which broadcasts over C-V2X to an onboard unit and an in-vehicle display I built — now installed in a test vehicle.
